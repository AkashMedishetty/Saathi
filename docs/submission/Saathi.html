01 · Project idea

A companion that sits on top of every app — for every kind of user

The person says what they want, the way they would ask a grandchild — “video call my son”, “find my Aadhaar and send it to him”, “Akash sent me a flight ticket, when is it?” — and Saathi walks them through it on the real app: a warm glow around the exact button, one short sentence in their language, spoken aloud, and a Do it for me button when they just want it done. The same guide scales from a grandmother’s first video call (Saathi, offline) to a developer reaching a server over SSH from the phone (Saathi Pro).

02 · Problem statement

Digital India left millions one tap behind — elders first, but not only

Pensions, Aadhaar, train and flight tickets, bills, doctors, family — it all lives in smartphone apps now. Yet millions of parents and grandparents were never taught how these apps work, many read their own language far better than English, and every phone brand and every update moves things around. The same wall stops a much larger group every day: students setting up coding tools, shop owners editing reels, professionals reaching a server or a spreadsheet from their phone — anyone facing an app for the first time.

Every task needs someone else

A video call, sending a photo, bigger text, finding a document — each waits for a son, daughter or neighbour to be free. Asking again and again costs independence and dignity.

English-first, ever-changing apps

Menus and icons change with every update and brand. A step learned last month is somewhere else today. Almost all help is English and generic.

Fear of breaking something

One wrong tap can call a stranger, delete a chat or open a popup nobody can close — so many simply stop trying.

Scams aim at exactly this person

“Share your screen”, “install this app”, “tell me the OTP”. Those least sure of their phone are the easiest to trick, and remote “help” is how most of these frauds begin.

Why today’s tools don’t close the gap

  • Voice assistants run a handful of commands and answer questions — they don’t walk you through the app on your screen, don’t teach, and mostly need the cloud.
  • Screen readers are built for blind users; they read everything aloud but don’t know your goal.
  • Remote help from family needs someone free and online, and trains people to let others control their phone — the habit scammers exploit.
  • Video tutorials are generic, English, for another phone, and you can’t ask them anything.

03 · Detailed solution

Understand the goal. Show the next step. Keep them safe. For everyone.

Saathi is an Android accessibility-service overlay with a stack of on-device models. It reads the structure of whatever app is on screen (the same accessibility tree screen readers use), understands what the person wants, decides the next step and shows it on the real app — then watches the screen change and moves on. It ships as two apps from one codebase: Saathi (fully on-device, no internet permission — elders and everyday tasks) and Saathi Pro (the same guide plus a cloud model for open-ended, expert goals).

1
HearVoice in English, Hindi or Telugu (on-device speech), or typing. One big light, SOS always there.
2
UnderstandInstant phrase routes, verified by Gemma 3 1B on the NPU (~90 ms). Unclear? Gemma 4 on the GPU rewrites it (~0.8 s).
3
DecideApp maps for WhatsApp, YouTube, Settings, Photos, Play Store, Phone… (CPU, ms). Other apps: the GPU planner.
4
ShowGlow on the exact button + one sentence, spoken. Do it for me · Read again · mic · close.
5
GuardPolicy on every action: no money apps, no OTP/PIN; Send, Pay, Install and Call stay the person’s tap.

Key features

  • Guides on the real app — glows the actual button, recovers when the person wanders (“I’m lost” → where you are and the way back).
  • Teach me, or Do it for me — same task, two modes; the final risky tap is always theirs.
  • Three languages, mixed freely — speech, cards and answers; Telugu/Hindi names become English letters to find contacts (ఆకాష్ → akash).
  • Understands natural sentences — the NPU checks every route, the GPU rewrites unclear requests and plans in apps nobody mapped.
  • Reads documents — screen capture → OCR → Gemma 4: tickets, letters, forms, labels.
  • Writes for them — letters and replies composed on-device, typed into the box they are looking at.
  • Never loses the thread — questions mid-task are answered while the task waits; interrupted tasks come back with “Continue”.
  • Scam guard — warnings on scam SMS, APKs, remote-control apps, money apps opened during a call.
  • Live AI monitor — shows which engine ran each step and how long it took.
  • Saathi Pro — a wider audience — students, professionals and creators: the cloud picks the right app for any goal (SSH, video editing, spreadsheets), guides the install and plans every step with the same glow.

04 · The app

Calm, large, and in their language

Home. One light to tap and speak, three languages, Type instead, SOS. “Helper on · works offline”.
Listening in Telugu. Examples in their own language: “call Akash on video”, “play Hanuman Chalisa”.
Settings. Name, language, family contacts — changeable only with the phone’s own screen lock.

Designed for the person, not the phone

  • Warm paper colours, large serif text, one action per card.
  • The card never covers the button it points at, nor the keyboard.
  • Text size, voice speed, dim-around-the-glow and scam warnings in Settings.

Two apps, one switch

  • Saathi: fully on-device, no internet permission.
  • Saathi Pro: adds a cloud model (the user’s own OpenRouter key) for power-user tasks such as SSH or video editing.
  • “Use this Saathi” makes one active; the other steps aside.

10 · Highlight

Paper form helper — “help me fill this form”

Aadhaar enrolment form: field 1 of 8, the box glows, the value in big letters to copy
Not in the saved details (“Post Office”): “ask your family”
Bank form: “To be filled in by the bank” is left out; 5 fields for the person

Happens

Point the camera at a paper form — or share a photo of it from the gallery or WhatsApp (“Fill this form with Saathi”). ML Kit OCR on the phone finds each label and its box, then walks one field at a time: zoomed to the box, the box glows, the card says what to write and shows it in big letters. Back / Next / Read again. Saathi never writes on the paper and nothing leaves the phone.

Leaves out what isn’t theirs

Sections meant for someone else are skipped by position and heading: “To be filled in by the bank”, “Certifier: to be filled by the District Child Protection Officer”, “For office use”, signatures, stamps, photo boxes. Aadhaar, PAN, bank and card boxes are never filled — the person writes those.

Values come from “My details”

The answers are preset once in the app’s My details profile (opens behind the phone’s screen lock). In this demo the profile holds sample values — name “Akash”, house/street “None”, area “Neon”, city and district “Karimnagar” — “None” is itself a preset value, not an OCR mistake. Missing ones (middle name, post office) are said plainly, never guessed.

Online forms too

On any form inside an app or web page, “help me fill this form” walks the editable fields in reading order, glows each and offers to type the saved value. Password, OTP and PIN fields are recognised and never filled.

Learning

Saathi learns with the person — and from the family

Phone School. Every skill as a small lesson; progress comes from doing it.
“Watch me …” A family member shows a task once.

Fading help

Each skill is tracked: new → practising → “You can do this!”. After three successes Saathi lets the person try first and only glows if they get stuck — teaching, not replacing.

Teach once — “watch me”

Saathi records the taps and safe typed values of a task shown once (“watch me open the school app”) and saves it as a lesson, replayed later with glows, step by step, by name and by voice.

New apps & new things

“How do I use Spotify / Google Docs?” gives guided first steps on the real app. For apps no one mapped, Gemma 4 plans each step from what is on screen, with drift checks (“where am I?”).

How the “model learns”: models are not retrained on the phone. Saathi learns by storing lessons, labels and progress in private on-device memory; the models receive that memory as context, so the same Gemma plans better on this person’s phone the more it is used.

What “adding a skill” means

  • A skill = keywords + steps (or an instant action) in one registry.
  • An app route = screens described by what is on them (labels, ids, positions) + steps with slots — one file per app, tested against real captured screen trees.
  • A lesson = recorded once by the family, no code.

What it gets for free

  • Works by voice in three languages and with any phrasing (the models map paraphrases onto it).
  • Resumes after interruptions; answers doubts mid-task; recovers when lost.
  • Every safety rule applies automatically; unknown screens fall back to the planner.

08 · Safety & privacy — enforced in code on every action
  • Never types or stores passwords, OTP, PIN, card, CVV, Aadhaar, PAN or bank numbers.
  • Never presses Send, Pay, Install or Call — the final tap is always the person’s.
  • Never guides inside banking or UPI apps: “please do this yourself, or with someone you trust.”
  • Basic Saathi has no internet permission; the document index keeps only “photo → kind”.
  • Saathi Pro is a separate app; the key is the user’s own; personal data is redacted before any request.
  • Family contacts can only be changed with the phone’s screen lock.
“The banks are right to block unknown screen readers — that’s how elders get robbed. Saathi never works inside money apps; it protects the person on the way in.”
